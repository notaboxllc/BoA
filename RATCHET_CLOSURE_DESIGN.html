A polymerization closure for the membrane Brownian ratchet

Per-tip, gap- & force-gated monomer addition — replacing the ad-hoc stericPolyFactor

BoxOfActin  ·  design note  ·  2026-06-15  ·  context: lamellipodium P1–P3; Mogilner–Oster elastic Brownian ratchet

1. Why a closure, not emergence

The elastic ratchet's tip fluctuation lives on the monomer scale (\(\delta \approx 2.7\,\)nm). Our filaments are chains of rigid FilSegment rods, each spanning stdSegLength \(\approx\) 32–64 monomers, so the terminal segment is rigid over \(\sim\)90–170 nm. The tip therefore cannot fluctuate at the scale the ratchet needs, and joint bending only opens a membrane-normal gap for oblique filaments (the normal excursion per joint rotation scales as \(L\sin\theta\,d\theta\), vanishing at normal incidence). The mechanism is genuinely sub-grid. The disciplined response is a physically-parameterized closure — not a "flexible tip" hack, which would reintroduce free parameters, miss the density-dependent free length, destabilize the integrator, and still fail head-on filaments.

A closure does not kill emergence. Supplied as a per-tip law that depends on the tip's actual geometry and load, it leaves the interesting behavior — ±35° orientation selection, load-sharing, array treadmilling — to self-organize at the network level (which filaments grow vs. stall vs. cap).

2. The core relation — the penalty is on the deficit

The rate is gated by whether there is room for a monomer of length \(\delta\). If the barbed end already sits a gap \(g\) short of the membrane (measured along the addition direction), a thermal fluctuation only needs to open the remaining \((\delta-g)\), not the whole \(\delta\):

$$ k_{\text{poly}} \;=\; k_{on}\,c \,\cdot\, \begin{cases} 1, & g \ge \delta \quad(\text{room already exists — free rate})\\[6pt] \exp\!\left(-\dfrac{f\,(\delta - g)}{k_B T}\right), & 0 \le g < \delta \quad(\text{ratchet against load }f) \end{cases} $$

Limit checks:

  • \(g = 0\):   \(k_{\text{poly}} = k_{on}c\,\exp(-f\delta/k_BT)\) — recovers classic Mogilner–Oster.
  • \(g = \delta\):   \(k_{\text{poly}} = k_{on}c\) — fully unobstructed; the penalty turns off.
  • monotonic and continuous in \(g\) between — an existing gap raises the rate, exactly as expected.

Depolymerization is left load-independent (the load doesn't help remove a bound monomer), so the full force–velocity follows as

$$ v \;=\; \delta\,\big(k_{\text{poly}} - k_{off}\big), \qquad f_{\text{stall}}\,\delta \;=\; k_B T\,\ln\!\frac{c\,k_{on}}{k_{off}}, $$

i.e. the load can do work per monomer up to the polymerization free energy, then stalls — the energy is the chemical drive you already supply, not a new input.

3. Incidence angle enters through the geometry of "room"

The monomer adds along the filament axis \(\hat{u}\); what matters is the axial room before the tip meets the membrane. With \(\theta\) the angle from the membrane normal \(\hat{n}\),

$$ g_{\text{axial}} \;=\; \frac{g_{\perp}}{\cos\theta}, $$

so an oblique tip converts a given perpendicular gap into more axial room (a grazing tip slides along the wall rather than into it), while the compliance available to close the residual deficit is the angle-projected bending mode \(\sim\sin\theta\). Thus \(\theta\) enters twice — how much room a gap buys (\(\cos\theta\)) and how easily the deficit is closed (\(\sin\theta\)) — and both are computable per tip.

4. The payoff: geometry does most of the work

The gap \(g\) is essentially end2TipC, the tip clearance the model already computes. Critically, gap and contact force are anti-correlated: when a real gap exists the tip isn't touching the membrane, so \(f \approx 0\) and the rate is near-free without the closure doing anything. The analytic Boltzmann factor only matters in the thin regime where \(g < \delta\) and the tip is loaded — the last sub-monomer crunch.

This shrinks the closure to a small correction on top of geometry the grid resolves natively. The noisy per-tip force read — the main implementation risk — only enters in that sub-\(\delta\) sliver; everywhere else end2TipC alone gives the answer.

5. One honest subtlety — what end2TipC represents

Whether end2TipC is the instantaneous or the quasi-static mean gap depends on whether the tip's fast fluctuation is resolved. With the over-damped rigid segment it is effectively the mean gap (set by the balance of polymerization push and membrane load). So the clean reading is: use end2TipC as the resolved mean clearance, let geometry handle \(g \ge \delta\) for free, and let \(\exp(-f(\delta-g)/k_BT)\) supply precisely the sub-grid thermal wobble the rigid segment cannot generate, over the residual \((\delta-g)\). Geometry where the grid resolves it; analytic closure only for the unresolved fluctuation.

7. Where it slots in

Replace the stericPolyFactor multiplier in FilSegment.end2BiochemSim with this gate: compute \(g_{\text{axial}}\) from end2TipC and \(\cos\theta\); if \(g_{\text{axial}}\ge\delta\), no attenuation; else multiply the poly rate by \(\exp(-f(\delta-g_{\text{axial}})/k_BT)\). Crucially, the membrane collision must keep providing mechanical push-back but stop separately throttling polymerization — otherwise the load is double-counted. Default-off behind a flag so it can be A/B'd against the current soft proxy.

Net. A per-tip law gated on the real clearance, with the ratchet penalty applied only to the unresolved sub-monomer deficit and weighted by the local load and incidence angle. More faithful than stericPolyFactor, discretization-independent, and less reliant on the messy force read than a blanket \(\exp(-f\delta/k_BT)\) would be — because an existing gap, at whatever angle, does exactly what you said it must: it raises the polymerization probability, all the way to the free rate once it reaches a full monomer.
